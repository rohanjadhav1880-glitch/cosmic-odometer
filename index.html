How the Math Works

A breakdown of every astrophysical calculation driving the Cosmic Odometer.

🌍 1. Rotated (Spin)

Earth is not just a sphere, it is an oblate spheroid that completes a full rotation every 23 hours, 56 minutes, and 4 seconds. The speed at which you travel due to this rotation depends entirely on your latitude. At the equator (0°), you are moving at roughly 1,674 km/h (0.465 km/s). As you move closer to the poles, this speed drops to nearly zero. The app calculates your exact continuous angular velocity based on the latitude slider.

[Source: Standard Earth Geometry / WGS 84]

☀️ 2. Orbited Sun

Earth does not sit still; it is constantly in freefall around the Sun. To complete its massive elliptical orbit (one year), Earth must travel at an average velocity of roughly 107,000 km/h (29.78 km/s). Every second you are alive, you are being dragged nearly 30 kilometers through the vacuum of the inner solar system. This is your standard "yearly lap".

[Source: Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion]

🌌 3. Solar Travel

The Sun is caught in the immense gravitational pull of the Milky Way galaxy. It drags the Earth, the planets, and the entire Oort Cloud along with it as it orbits the supermassive black hole at the galactic center (Sagittarius A*). To complete its 225-million-year orbit, our entire solar system is hurtling through the Orion Arm at roughly 792,000 km/h (220 km/s).

[Source: International Astronomical Union (IAU)]

🚀 4. Galactic Travel

The Milky Way itself is not stationary. It is falling toward the Great Attractor and being pushed by the Dipole Repeller. When measured against the absolute rest frame of the universe (the Cosmic Microwave Background radiation left over from the Big Bang), our entire galaxy is moving through deep space at a staggering ~2.1 million km/h (600 km/s).

[Source: COBE / WMAP / Planck Satellite Data]

⏱️ Time Dilation

According to Einstein's theory of Special Relativity, the faster you move through space, the slower you move through time. Because Earth is flying through the universe at 600 km/s, your biological clock is ticking slightly slower than someone sitting completely still outside the galaxy. We calculate your exact "time debt" using the Lorentz Factor.

[Source: Lorentz Factor / Special Relativity]

🧠 Micro-Relativity

Einstein's theory of General Relativity proves that gravity also slows down time (gravitational time dilation). Because your feet are closer to the Earth's center of mass than your head is, time literally moves slower for your feet.Using the precise formula Δt = t * (g * h) / c², we calculate the nanosecond difference in age between your head and toes (assuming an average global height of 1.7 meters)

[Source: Einstein's General Relativity (NIST optical clock experiments)]

💥 Universal Expansion

The universe is not just expanding; the expansion is accelerating due to Dark Energy. The fabric of space itself is stretching, pushing distant objects away from us. We use the Hubble Constant (roughly 70 km/s/Mpc) to calculate that the nearby Virgo Supercluster is actively being pushed away from Earth at roughly 1,150 km/s.

[Source: The Hubble Constant (H0) ~70 km/s/Mpc]

☄️ Galactic Collision

While the broader universe expands outward, objects that are close together are gravitationally bound. Our closest galactic neighbor, the Andromeda Galaxy, is locked in a gravitational dance with the Milky Way. It is actively falling toward us at roughly 110 km/s. This counter tracks exactly how much closer it has gotten to hitting us since you were born.

[Source: Hubble Space Telescope / Gaia Telemetry]

🏃 Cosmic Horizon

Because the expansion of the universe is accelerating, distant galaxies are eventually pushed away from us faster than the speed of light. Once this happens, their light can never reach Earth again, and they slip over the "Cosmological Event Horizon." We estimate that roughly 22,000 stars disappear from our observable universe every single second.

[Source: Cosmological Event Horizon approximations]

💥 Supernovae

Stars are constantly dying. In our observable universe, a massive star runs out of fuel and detonates in a catastrophic supernova explosion roughly every 30 seconds. This live counter tracks the estimated total number of star deaths that have illuminated the universe during your specific lifetime.

[Source: Vera C. Rubin Observatory / Cosmic Star Formation History]

👻 Neutrino Shower

The nuclear fusion happening inside the Sun constantly blasts out ghostly, near-massless particles called neutrinos. They pass right through solid matter. Roughly 100 trillion solar neutrinos pass through every square centimeter of Earth per second. We calculate how many pass through the ~10,000 cm² surface area of an average human body.

[Source: Standard Solar Model (SSM)]

🫀 The Human Engine

To contrast the massive scales of the cosmos with the microscopic scales of human biology, this engine tracks the physical work your body has done to keep you alive. Based on medical averages, it tracks your heartbeats (~69 bpm), breaths (~15 per minute), and the staggering ~3.8 million cells your body replaces every single second.

[Source: Human Biology / Medical Averages]

🔭 Lookback Telescope

Because the speed of light is finite, looking into space is literally looking back in time. The light reflecting off Earth on the day you were born has been traveling outward ever since. Your exact age in years is the radius (in Light-Years) of your "life bubble". We cross-reference this radius with real star distances to see where an alien would need to be to watch you being born right now.

[Source: Speed of Light (c) / Hipparcos Star Catalog]

⚡ Photon Travel

The absolute speed limit of the universe is the speed of light (299,792.458 km/s). This metric ignores the physical movement of the Earth and instead calculates exactly how far a single photon, emitted from Earth at the exact millisecond of your birth, has traveled through the vacuum of space.

[Source: Special Relativity / c constant]
